### 5. KEY MECHANISMS of AEVO: How does it self-evolve?

AEVO introduces a novel approach to self-evolution by shifting the focus from directly generating better solutions to intelligently modifying the *mechanism that generates solutions*. This is achieved through a "meta-editing framework" within an "interactive environment."

1.  **Agentic Evolution as an Interactive Environment:** AEVO conceptualizes the entire evolutionary process (including candidate generation, evaluation, and feedback loops) as an "interactive environment." Within this environment, the "accumulated evolution context" (which includes candidates, traces, and failures) is treated as a process-level state.
2.  **Meta-Agent:** A specialized "meta-agent" is at the core of AEVO. Unlike a regular agent that produces solutions, the meta-agent operates at a higher level of abstraction.
3.  **Observation of State:** The meta-agent continuously *observes* this process-level state. It gathers comprehensive information from the evolution context, specifically:
    *   `candidates`: The proposed solutions or artifacts.
    *   `traces`: The execution paths or logs of how candidates were generated and evaluated.
    *   `failures`: Information about why certain candidates or evolutionary attempts failed.
4.  **Meta-Editing (Adaptive Evolution):** Instead of directly generating the next candidate, the meta-agent performs a "meta-edit." This involves modifying the *procedure or agent context* that governs how future candidates are generated and evaluated. This is the core "self-evolution" mechanism: the system is changing its own underlying rules and strategies.
5.  **Run Plan and Evolution Segment:** After a meta-edit, the meta-agent sets a "run plan" for the *newly modified evolution mechanism*. This new mechanism then runs for an "evolution segment" (multiple rounds). This allows the meta-agent to observe the effects of its changes over a sustained period, rather than making micro-adjustments per candidate.
6.  **Continuous Feedback Loop:** The new evidence generated during the "evolution segment" (new candidates, traces, failures) is then accumulated and fed back to the meta-agent as the updated "state" for the *next* meta-edit. This forms a continuous, adaptive feedback loop, allowing the system to learn and improve its own evolutionary strategy over time.
7.  **Addressing Limitations of Prior Methods:** This meta-level control addresses the limitations of traditional fixed-procedure methods (which are rigid) and existing agent-based methods (which can "drift" in long-horizon evolution due to unorganized evidence). AEVO provides a stable interface to organize evidence and systematically revise the evolutionary mechanism itself.

### 6. SPECIFIC TECHNIQUES for self-modification.

AEVO offers two main variants, each modifying different aspects of the underlying evolution process:

1.  **AEVO Procedure:**
    *   In this variant, the meta-agent directly modifies the "procedure code" that defines the evolutionary algorithm. This is akin to an agent writing or revising its own algorithmic steps.
    *   Specific elements of the procedure code that can be modified include:
        *   `selection`: The rules or criteria for selecting promising candidates for further refinement or to form the next "generation."
        *   `optimizer`: The specific optimization algorithm or strategy being used (e.g., how to search the solution space, mutation rates, crossover strategies).
        *   `feedback summary`: How the system processes, aggregates, and summarizes evaluation feedback to extract actionable insights.
        *   `budget`: The allocation of computational resources or iteration limits for different stages of the evolution.

2.  **AEVO Agent:**
    *   In this variant, the meta-agent modifies the "operating context" of the inner agent that performs the evolutionary tasks. This is about adjusting the capabilities and directives of the agent rather than its explicit code.
    *   Specific aspects of the operating context that can be modified include:
        *   `skills`: The set of tools, functions, or inherent capabilities the inner agent can utilize. This could involve adding new tools, refining existing ones, or changing their priorities.
        *   `goals`: The specific objectives or sub-goals that guide the inner agent's behavior during the evolution process. The meta-agent can refine, adjust, or reprioritize these goals.
        *   `memory`: How the inner agent stores, accesses, and utilizes past experiences or information. This could involve modifying memory structures, retention policies, or retrieval mechanisms.
        *   `format feedback`: How the inner agent interprets and processes the feedback it receives from the evaluation environment. This ensures the agent effectively understands and incorporates learning signals.
