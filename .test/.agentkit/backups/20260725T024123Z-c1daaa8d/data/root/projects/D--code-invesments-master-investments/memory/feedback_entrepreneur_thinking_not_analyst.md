260320 brainstorm session — user explicitly called out that ALL analysis (3 parallel research agents + synthesis) was "tư duy chuyên môn thay vì doanh nhân" (specialist thinking instead of entrepreneur thinking).

**Specific failures:**
1. Probability tables for business models = analyst thinking, not entrepreneur thinking
2. "Path 1 vs Path 2 vs Path 3" generic comparison = consultant framework
3. No SPECIFIC market opportunities identified — just categories
4. Missing the "find opportunity in mass market" mindset
5. No real cash flow mechanism design — just CCC numbers from internet
6. "Pieter Levels model" = describing what someone else did, not designing what YOU should do
7. Owner efficiency section = productivity advice, not strategic leverage thinking

**What real entrepreneurs do differently:**
- They find SPECIFIC arbitrage: where people pay $X for something that AI/tech can deliver for $0.01X
- They intercept existing money flow, not create new demand
- They think in MECHANISMS: "if I do X, money flows because Y" — not "this model has 15% probability"
- They execute THIS WEEK, not "Month 1-3 phase planning"
- They see what NOBODY else sees in the same data
- Their decisions create asymmetric payoffs: small downside, massive upside

**Why:** The entire agent system defaults to analyst/consultant templates. This produces content that looks professional but has zero decision-making value for someone who actually needs to make $1M/month.

**How to apply:**
- NEVER present probability tables for business models
- NEVER do "Path 1 vs 2 vs 3" generic comparisons
- ALWAYS identify SPECIFIC opportunities (company name, market, price gap, exact play)
- ALWAYS design the cash flow MECHANISM (how does $1 become $2, step by step)
- ALWAYS think: "What would I actually DO Monday morning with $30K?"
- Test: Would Marc Rich, Pieter Levels, or Alex Hormozi say "yeah, that's a real play"? If not, it's too generic.
- The analysis must reveal something the user couldn't get from 5 minutes of googling
